# Is this a real problem? Evidence from real freelancers

Collected Oct 20, 2026 from public posts and reports. Each item links to its source. Items marked *(search snippet)* were seen only in search results because the site blocked direct reading; check them before quoting.

## Real stories

1. **Web developer:** a client paid $1.5k upfront for a dental-chain website, then reversed the PayPal charge just before handoff. PayPal sided with the developer, but the card issuer sided with the client, leaving the developer's account $1,500 negative. The real reason came out later: the client had decided to have GoDaddy build the site instead. [Hacker News, 2014](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7365028)
2. **Project freelancer:** the client paid the final milestone, received the work, then filed a card chargeback: "The CC company ruled in his favor, and Paypal offered no way to dispute." Left at −$1,500. [Hacker News, 2014](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=8735062)
3. **Upwork developer:** asked to refund $12,500 because the client had paid with someone else's card. Filed as "unauthorized"; the client admitted he forgot the card wasn't his. The thread drew 1,142 points. [Hacker News, 2021](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29862860) ([original post](https://alanany.com/2021/12/23/upwork-12k-refund-client-using-another-credit-card/), now paywalled)
4. **Upwork, secondhand:** a client paid $40k by Amex, then charged it back; the platform debited the freelancer as soon as the chargeback arrived. [Hacker News comment, 2022](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29865362)
5. **Freelance writer:** paid $125 for five articles. The client thanked her and published them, then filed a chargeback: "The $125 has been removed from my account and I've been charged a $20 fee!" [The Write Life](https://thewritelife.com/chargebacks/)
6. **Creator agency (India):** new clients paid for video and thumbnail work, then requested chargebacks days later. PayPal refunded them without contacting the agency; losses passed 80k INR. [TechEnclave, 2025](https://techenclave.com/t/advice-paypal-negative-balance/275723)
7. **Upwork freelancer:** two contracts closed with 5-star feedback, then "after 11 days… the client has filed a chargeback on both projects." *(search snippet)* [Upwork Community, 2024](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Support-Forum/Client-filed-Chargeback-after-close-both-contracts/m-p/1573109)
8. **Illustrator Tom Richmond:** relays a case where a client won a PayPal dispute over delivered artwork and kept using it on their website, and notes PayPal's usual proof (delivery confirmation, photos) doesn't fit creative work. [Blog, 2010](https://tomrichmond.com/the-dangers-of-accepting-paypal-or-credit-cards/09/12/2010/)
9. **PayPal Community threads** *(titles from search)*: ["Lost case on virtual product but client received it and uses it"](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Managing-Risk-and-Fraud-Archives/Lost-case-on-virtual-product-but-client-received-it-and-uses-it/td-p/1971522), ["HELP, NEGATIVE BALANCE, I'm a freelancer and my client decided to…"](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Security-and-Fraud-Archives/HELP-NEGATIVE-BALANCE-I-m-a-freelancer-and-my-client-decided-to/td-p/3060480)

## Numbers

- **Friendly fraud is ~44% of chargebacks merchants absorb; average net recovery is only 10.7%; 29% of merchants say collecting evidence is their biggest obstacle.** Chargebacks911 2026 Chargeback Field Report, via [Accounts Recovery](https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2026/09/01/friendly-fraud-accounts-for-44-of-chargebacks-chargebacks911-report-finds/)
- **Merchants win only 18% of the chargebacks they appeal.** Chargebacks911 2024 survey, via [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/12/234590-nearly-half-of-chargebacks-stem-from-misuse-or-outright-abuse-report/)
- **72% of cardholders see a chargeback as a valid alternative to asking the seller for a refund.** [PayPal, 2024](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/what-is-friendly-fraud), citing Chargebacks911
- **US merchants lose $4.61 for every $1 of fraud.** LexisNexis True Cost of Fraud 2025, via [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/04/238132-e-commerce-fraud-costly-lexisnexis-risk-solutions/)
- **70% of surveyed US freelancers get paid through PayPal**, the most-cited method. [PayPal Freelancer Insights, 2018](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/PayPal-Releases-Global-Freelancer-Insights)
- **64 million Americans freelanced in 2023 (38% of the workforce).** [Upwork Freelance Forward 2023](https://www.upwork.com/press/releases/upwork-study-finds-64-million-americans-freelanced-in-2023-adding-1-27-trillion-to-u-s-economy) *(search snippet)*

## Patterns (why our approach fits)

- **The stated reason often isn't the real one.** "Unauthorized" hid a client using a card that wasn't his; a reversal hid a switch to another provider; a "low quality" writer was thanked and published.
- **The client often keeps using the work**, which is strong evidence, but freelancers rarely know how to present it.
- **Losses go beyond the payment:** negative balances, dispute fees, platforms debiting before any ruling.
- **Proving delivery of a service is the hard part.** There's no tracking number; the proof is scattered across contracts, emails and logs. That's exactly what the agent assembles.

## Ask freelancers directly

**Post for r/freelance** (from your own account; disclose it's a student project):

> **Has a client ever reversed a payment after approving your work?**
>
> Hi all! We're a student team building a project for a hackathon, and we're trying to understand what happens when a client files a PayPal dispute or card chargeback after you've delivered and they've signed off.
>
> If it's happened to you: what reason did they give, and was it the real one? How long did you have to respond, did you know what evidence to send, and did you get the money back?
>
> Anonymous stories are totally fine, and we won't quote anyone without permission. Thanks for sharing. Hoping this helps others avoid it too.

**5-question survey** (Google Form):

1. Has a client ever disputed or charged back a payment after you delivered work? (Yes, once / Yes, more than once / No / Not sure)
2. What reason did they officially give? (Not as described / Unauthorized / Not received / Other / Don't know)
3. What do you believe the real reason was? (Miscommunication / Change of staff or decision-maker / Buyer's remorse or budget / Didn't receive the files / Intentional fraud / Other)
4. What was the outcome? (Won / Lost / Partial refund or settlement / Didn't respond / Still open)
5. How confident were you about what evidence to submit? (1–5), plus an optional box: how long did you have to respond?
